## Research Question:
How does logit-space noise (characterized by mean μ and std σ) affect 
the robustness of Semantic Entropy as an uncertainty measure?

## Methodology:
- Y-axis: Relative SE Change = (SE_before - SE_after) / SE_before
  - Positive values → SE decreases (less uncertain after noise)
  - Negative values → SE increases (more uncertain after noise)
  - Zero → No change (perfectly robust)
- X-axis: Noise parameters (μ and σ)

## ANALYSIS & OBSERVATIONS:

### 1. EFFECT OF MEAN NOISE (μ) - Plot 1
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Data Summary:
  μ = 0.0:  Mean Δ = +0.00% (±52.0%)
  μ = 0.5:  Mean Δ = -0.08% (±51.4%)
  μ = 1.0:  Mean Δ = -0.08% (±52.0%)
  μ = 2.0:  Mean Δ = +0.50% (±54.1%)

KEY OBSERVATION #1: Mean noise has NEGLIGIBLE systematic effect
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
✓ All mean changes are within ±0.5% (essentially zero)
✓ SE remains centered around 0% change across all μ values
✓ The relative change is INVARIANT to bias in logit perturbations

INTERPRETATION:
- Adding constant bias to logits does NOT systematically shift SE
- SE is robust to mean shifts in the probability distribution
- Whether noise is centered (μ=0) or biased (μ=2), SE behavior is similar

IMPLICATION:
→ Semantic Entropy is BIAS-INVARIANT: It captures semantic clustering,
  not absolute probability levels, making it robust to calibration shifts.


### 2. EFFECT OF STANDARD DEVIATION (σ) - Plot 2
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

  σ = 1.0:  Mean Δ = +1.09% (±50.5%)  [SE decreased]

KEY OBSERVATION #2: Std noise shows NON-LINEAR effect
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

⚠ High noise (σ=2.0): SE INCREASES (-3% trend)
⚠ Variance increases with σ (50.6% → 55.8%)

INTERPRETATION:
1. At low noise levels (σ ≤ 1.0):
   - Small perturbations may slightly SHARPEN semantic clusters
   - Acts like "regularization", marginally reducing entropy
   - Effect is minimal (+2% = negligible in practice)

2. At high noise levels (σ = 2.0):
   - Large perturbations BEGIN to DEGRADE semantic clustering
   - Answers that were semantically similar may drift apart
   - SE starts increasing (entropy grows), but effect is still small (-3%)

3. Variance grows with σ:
   - Higher noise → more unpredictable SE changes across questions
   - Some questions become more robust, others less robust

OBSERVATION #4: Overall Robustness
